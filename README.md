# REBUILD

### A proposed technology-enabled survivor-support and reintegration initiative

> REBUILD is an early-stage idea focused on helping survivors of severe
> interpersonal violence and their families find safe, verified human support
> and pathways toward rebuilding their lives.

## Current Status

REBUILD is currently in the **idea and research stage**.

The initial plan is to understand the existing support ecosystem, consult NGOs
and trained professionals, identify gaps, and gradually develop a focused,
privacy-conscious prototype. No operational platform has been launched yet.

The initiative is being explored as a student-led project, with the possibility
of developing partnerships with established NGOs, professionals, volunteers,
and other organisations in the future.

## The Problem

Survivors of acid attacks, domestic violence, sexual violence, and other forms
of severe interpersonal violence may face several barriers after an incident,
including:

- Difficulty finding reliable support
- Fragmented medical, legal, psychological, and rehabilitation services
- Financial dependence
- Interrupted education or employment
- Social stigma and isolation
- Lack of clear information about available assistance
- Difficulty accessing legal or government support
- Repeatedly having to explain their situation to different organisations
- Lack of safe and trustworthy ways to request help

Existing organisations already provide valuable support. REBUILD aims to
explore whether technology can make these services easier and safer to find,
access, and coordinate.

## Proposed Vision

To help survivors and their families move from crisis toward recovery,
rehabilitation, independence, and social reintegration—with dignity, privacy,
choice, and human support.

REBUILD would focus on survivors’ agency and future possibilities rather than
defining them permanently by the violence they experienced.

## Proposed First Focus

The first version would focus on helping survivors or family members request
support and connect with verified NGOs and trained professionals.

The platform would not replace counsellors, lawyers, doctors, police, or
existing support organisations. It would act as a support-navigation and
referral layer.

The proposed journey is:

**Crisis → Recovery → Rehabilitation → Education and Skills → Employment →
Independence → Social Reintegration**

## Initial MVP

The first minimum viable product would be a **verified, consent-based
survivor-support and referral platform**.

It would focus on one core user journey:

> A survivor or trusted family member requests help and is connected with an
> appropriate, verified organisation or trained professional.

### 1. Support-request system

- A simple, accessible help-request form
- Requests submitted by survivors, family members, or trusted individuals
- Emergency, urgent, and non-urgent categories
- Selection of the type of support needed
- Consent-based communication preferences
- Guidance for bystanders and family members on helping safely
- No uploading of survivor photographs or personal information without
  appropriate consent

### 2. Verified support directory

The platform would aim to maintain a carefully reviewed directory of:

- NGOs and survivor-support organisations
- Shelters and safe accommodation
- Medical and reconstructive-care services
- Psychological counselling services
- Legal-aid organisations and lawyers
- Appropriate police and emergency contacts
- Government rehabilitation and compensation schemes
- Education and scholarship programmes
- Skills-training organisations
- Employment and entrepreneurship opportunities
- Financial-assistance programmes
- Disability-support and accessibility services

Each listing could include:

- Services provided
- Eligibility requirements
- Geographic coverage
- Languages supported
- Whether the service is free or paid
- Operating hours
- Safe contact methods
- Date of last verification

### 3. Needs-based referrals

- Matching support requests with relevant organisations
- Referrals based on location, urgency, and support requirements
- Referral creation and assignment
- Referral acceptance, rejection, or redirection by authorised organisations
- Consent tracking
- Referral-status updates
- Follow-up reminders
- Escalation pathways for urgent cases
- Referral history to reduce the need for survivors to repeatedly explain their
  situation

### 4. NGO and professional dashboard

Authorised partner organisations could have access to:

- Referral requests
- Case categories and priority levels
- Consent status
- Assigned staff members
- Referral status
- Follow-up reminders
- Service availability
- Referral outcomes
- Secure communication tools
- Aggregate impact statistics
- Audit logs

NGOs and trained professionals—not the platform itself—would manage cases and
provide specialised support.

### 5. Safety, privacy, and safeguarding

The platform would be designed around:

- Informed consent
- Minimal data collection
- Survivor-controlled communication preferences
- Role-based access controls
- Secure authentication
- Encryption where appropriate
- Restricted access to sensitive information
- Clear data-retention and deletion policies
- Reporting of harassment, impersonation, threats, and privacy violations
- Human review of sensitive decisions
- Protection against unauthorised sharing of survivor information
- Safety procedures for high-risk and emergency situations

Survivor identities, photographs, addresses, medical information, and other
sensitive details would not be publicly displayed by default.

### 6. Basic recovery-navigation pathway

The MVP could provide structured information about possible next steps, such as:

1. Immediate safety
2. Medical assistance
3. Psychological support
4. Legal guidance
5. Shelter or housing
6. Documentation and government benefits
7. Education or skills training
8. Employment or entrepreneurship
9. Financial independence
10. Social reintegration

This would be a navigation and information tool, not an automated medical or
legal decision-making system.

### 7. Basic impact tracking

The platform could measure non-identifying indicators such as:

- Number of verified organisations
- Number of support requests
- Number of referrals made
- Referral response time
- Referral completion rate
- Types of services accessed
- Number of active partner organisations
- User and partner feedback
- Privacy or safeguarding incidents

## Future Roadmap

The following features would be explored only after research, consultation,
safety testing, and partnership development.

### 1. Financial assistance and donations

REBUILD could explore a safeguarded assistance system for verified needs,
including:

- Medical treatment
- Rehabilitation
- Safe accommodation
- Education
- Vocational training
- Legal expenses
- Essential living costs
- Assistive devices

Possible features could include:

- Funding requests verified by partner NGOs
- Donations managed through trusted organisations
- Optional donor anonymity
- Purpose-based contributions
- Human review before funds are released
- Transparent records of amounts received and disbursed
- Anonymised financial reports
- Fraud-prevention measures
- Complaint and dispute procedures
- Refund procedures where appropriate
- Impact updates that do not reveal survivor identities

Survivors would not be required to publish their names, photographs, or
personal stories to receive assistance.

### 2. Financial transparency and blockchain research

Blockchain may be explored as a future tool for:

- Tamper-evident donation records
- Disbursement audit trails
- Verification of financial transactions
- Public aggregate financial reporting
- Cryptographic proofs of selected records

However, sensitive information would never be placed on a public blockchain.
Names, photographs, addresses, medical details, case histories, and identifying
information would remain in secure, access-controlled systems.

A conventional auditable database would likely be used first. Blockchain would
only be introduced if it solves a clearly identified transparency problem.

### 3. Education, employment, and economic independence

Future development could include:

- Scholarship listings
- Education and training opportunities
- Skills-development programmes
- Employment opportunities from inclusive employers
- Internship opportunities
- Mentorship
- Resume and interview support
- Entrepreneurship resources
- Microfinance and financial-literacy referrals
- Workplace accommodation information
- Employer partnerships
- Long-term independence pathways

### 4. Moderated survivor peer support

REBUILD could eventually explore safe, moderated spaces where survivors can
connect with others who have had similar experiences.

Possible safeguards would include:

- Opt-in participation
- Pseudonymous participation
- Trained moderators
- Appropriate identity and safety checks
- Reporting and blocking tools
- No unmoderated random matching
- Restrictions on recording and sharing
- Crisis-escalation procedures
- Clear rules against harassment, exploitation, and victim-blaming
- Professional safeguarding partnerships

This feature would not be launched without substantial research and
appropriate support structures.

### 5. Legal and justice navigation

Future features could include:

- Information about legal-aid services
- Connections with verified legal professionals
- Information about compensation and rehabilitation schemes
- Document checklists
- Guidance on where to seek protection or report abuse
- Appointment and referral reminders
- Information about relevant government authorities
- Safe legal-resource navigation

REBUILD would not replace lawyers, courts, police, or qualified legal
professionals.

### 6. Awareness and social change

A future awareness programme could focus on:

- Challenging victim-blaming
- Promoting survivor dignity
- Educating people about consent and abuse
- Teaching safe bystander intervention
- Helping families understand how to support survivors
- Awareness programmes in schools and colleges
- Training for volunteers, employers, and community leaders
- Information about survivor rights and available services
- Encouraging responsibility and accountability for perpetrators

The initiative would emphasise that responsibility for violence lies with the
perpetrator, not the survivor.

### 7. Survivor and family guidance

Future resources could help families and trusted individuals understand:

- How to support a survivor without taking away their agency
- How to protect privacy
- How to avoid victim-blaming
- How to identify urgent risks
- How to approach NGOs and professionals
- How to help with documentation and applications
- How to support education, employment, and rehabilitation

### 8. Public safety and policy research

REBUILD may eventually explore research and advocacy related to:

- Survivor-protection mechanisms
- Protection from stalking and retaliation
- Legally authorised offender-monitoring systems
- Rehabilitation and compensation policies
- Institutional accountability
- Safer reporting and referral systems

Any offender-related information would require lawful, verified, and legally
reviewed sources. REBUILD would not independently publish alleged offenders’
faces or personal information, and it would not encourage vigilantism.

## Guiding Principles

REBUILD would be guided by:

- Survivor dignity and agency
- Informed consent
- Privacy and confidentiality
- Safety before convenience
- Human oversight
- Accessibility and inclusion
- Transparency and accountability
- Collaboration with established organisations
- Data minimisation
- Non-discrimination
- Respect for survivor choice
- Professional safeguarding

## Expected Social Impact

The long-term goal is to make it easier for survivors and their families to
find trustworthy assistance and move toward recovery, independence, and social
reintegration.

Potential impact could include:

- Easier access to reliable support information
- Faster and more appropriate referrals
- Better coordination between organisations
- Improved access to medical, legal, and psychological support
- Greater access to rehabilitation services
- Increased access to education and employment opportunities
- Safer and more accountable financial assistance
- Reduced dependence on informal or unreliable sources of information
- Greater awareness of survivor rights
- Reduced victim-blaming and social stigma
- Increased feelings of support, dignity, and agency

The intended impact is not simply to provide information, but to help people
feel that rebuilding their lives is possible.

A meaningful sign of success would be when a survivor who once felt that their
life had ended can say:

> “I want to live again.”

## Development Approach

The proposed development process is:

1. Research existing organisations and support systems
2. Consult NGOs, legal professionals, healthcare workers, and trained
   safeguarding professionals
3. Identify a focused and validated problem
4. Map available services and existing gaps
5. Design a safe and accessible prototype
6. Test the system in a controlled environment
7. Establish appropriate partnerships
8. Evaluate usefulness, safety, and accessibility
9. Expand gradually based on evidence and feedback

## Proposed Six-Month Direction

### Month 1: Research and ecosystem mapping

- Study existing support systems
- Identify relevant organisations
- Consult NGOs and trained professionals
- Map available services and gaps
- Develop safeguarding and privacy principles

### Month 2: MVP design

- Define the first user journey
- Design the information architecture
- Create wireframes
- Define user roles and access levels
- Develop the data-protection model
- Establish initial partner requirements

### Month 3: Prototype development

- Build the support-request form
- Develop the verified service directory
- Build the initial NGO dashboard
- Implement referral workflows
- Add basic authentication and access controls

### Month 4: Testing and partner preparation

- Conduct usability testing
- Perform privacy and security testing
- Review the platform with professionals
- Develop referral procedures
- Verify potential partner organisations
- Test emergency and high-risk scenarios through simulations

### Month 5: Controlled pilot

- Work with a small number of trusted organisations
- Test the referral process in a limited setting
- Collect partner feedback
- Monitor referral outcomes
- Identify and fix safety or usability problems

### Month 6: Evaluation and next-stage planning

- Evaluate the pilot
- Measure referral and response outcomes
- Review safeguarding performance
- Publish anonymised findings
- Improve the prototype
- Prepare a roadmap for future partnerships and features

## Possible Success Indicators

During the early stage, success could be measured through:

- A verified directory of relevant organisations
- A small group of active NGO or professional partners
- A functioning and tested prototype
- A controlled pilot
- Successful supervised referrals
- Improved access to reliable support information
- Positive feedback from partner organisations
- Strong privacy and safeguarding performance
- Clear evidence about which features are genuinely useful
