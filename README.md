# REBUILD

### A proposed technology-enabled survivor-support and reintegration initiative

> REBUILD is a student-led, early-stage initiative idea focused on helping
> survivors of acid attacks, domestic violence, sexual violence, and other
> forms of severe interpersonal violence find safe, verified human support
> and pathways toward rebuilding their lives.

---

## Current Status

REBUILD is currently in the **idea and research stage**.

The proposed six-month plan is to study existing survivor-support systems,
consult NGOs and trained professionals, identify genuine gaps, and develop a
small, privacy-conscious prototype. No operational platform has been launched
yet.

The initiative may eventually involve NGOs, legal and healthcare
professionals, safeguarding specialists, trained community coordinators,
technical contributors, volunteers, and responsible donors.

---

## The Problem

Survivors and their families may need medical care, counselling, legal
assistance, safe accommodation, rehabilitation, education, employment, or
financial support after an incident. However, finding appropriate help can be
difficult when services are fragmented across different organisations.

Survivors may also face:

- Physical and psychological trauma
- Financial dependence
- Interrupted education or careers
- Social stigma and isolation
- Victim-blaming
- Difficulty understanding legal and government-support systems
- Lack of safe and reliable information
- Repeatedly explaining their experiences to different organisations
- Uncertainty about whom to trust

At the same time, people who want to help may not know how to do so safely,
and donors may struggle to identify trustworthy organisations or verified
needs.

Existing organisations already provide valuable services. REBUILD aims to
explore whether technology can make these services easier and safer to find,
access, coordinate, and support.

---

## Proposed Vision

To help survivors and their families move from:

**Crisis → Recovery → Rehabilitation → Education and Skills → Employment →
Independence → Social Reintegration**

REBUILD would focus on survivor dignity, agency, privacy, consent, safety, and
long-term independence rather than defining people permanently by the violence
they experienced.

---

## Proposed First Focus

The initial focus is a **verified, consent-based survivor-support and
referral platform**.

A survivor or trusted family member could request help and be connected with
appropriate NGOs, trained professionals, or verified support services.

REBUILD would not replace:

- Doctors
- Counsellors
- Lawyers
- Police
- Emergency services
- Social workers
- Existing survivor-support organisations

Instead, it would act as a support-navigation, referral, and coordination
layer.

# Initial Six-Month MVP

The first six months would focus on research, safeguarding, prototype
development, professional consultation, and a controlled pilot.

The MVP would not attempt to implement every long-term feature immediately.

## 1. Support-request system

- Accessible support-request form
- Requests submitted by survivors, family members, or trusted individuals
- Emergency, urgent, and non-urgent categories
- Selection of the type of help required
- Basic location or service-area information
- Consent-based communication preferences
- Option to request support without publicly revealing identity
- Guidance for family members and bystanders
- Clear instructions for situations requiring emergency services
- Secure request handling
- Human review of sensitive or high-risk requests

## 2. Verified support directory

The platform would aim to maintain a reviewed directory of:

- NGOs
- Survivor-support organisations
- Shelters
- Safe accommodation providers
- Medical facilities
- Reconstructive-care services
- Psychological counselling services
- Legal-aid organisations
- Verified legal professionals
- Appropriate police and emergency contacts
- Government rehabilitation schemes
- Government compensation schemes
- Education and scholarship programmes
- Skills-training organisations
- Employment-support organisations
- Entrepreneurship-support programmes
- Financial-assistance programmes
- Disability-support and accessibility services

Each listing could include:

- Services provided
- Eligibility requirements
- Geographic coverage
- Languages supported
- Whether the service is free or paid
- Operating hours
- Safe contact methods
- Referral requirements
- Date of last verification
- Verification status

## 3. Needs-based referral system

- Matching requests with relevant support organisations
- Matching based on support type, location, urgency, and eligibility
- Referral creation and assignment
- Referral acceptance, rejection, or redirection
- Consent tracking
- Referral-status updates
- Follow-up reminders
- Referral completion tracking
- Escalation pathways for urgent cases
- Referral history
- Reduced need for survivors to repeatedly explain their situation
- Human approval for sensitive referrals

## 4. NGO and professional dashboard

Authorised organisations could access:

- Referral requests
- Case categories
- Case priority
- Consent status
- Assigned staff member
- Referral status
- Follow-up reminders
- Service availability
- Referral outcomes
- Secure communication
- Case notes where strictly necessary
- Aggregate impact statistics
- Audit logs

Access would be restricted according to each organisation’s role and
responsibilities.

## 5. Basic recovery-navigation pathway

The platform could guide users toward relevant categories of support:

1. Immediate safety
2. Medical assistance
3. Psychological support
4. Legal guidance
5. Shelter or housing
6. Documentation
7. Government benefits
8. Rehabilitation
9. Education
10. Skills development
11. Employment
12. Entrepreneurship
13. Financial independence
14. Social reintegration

This would provide navigation and information, not automated medical or legal
decisions.

## 6. Consent, privacy, and safeguarding system

The MVP would prioritise:

- Informed consent
- Minimal data collection
- Survivor-controlled communication preferences
- Role-based access control
- Secure authentication
- Encryption where appropriate
- Restricted access to sensitive information
- Data-retention and deletion policies
- Consent withdrawal procedures
- Reporting of harassment, impersonation, threats, or privacy violations
- Human review of sensitive decisions
- High-risk case escalation
- Protection against unauthorised sharing
- Safety procedures for in-person contact
- Clear boundaries for volunteers and contributors

Survivor names, photographs, addresses, medical details, and other identifying
information would not be publicly displayed by default.

## 7. Basic NGO verification system

- Verification records for partner organisations
- Review of services and eligibility claims
- Verification dates
- Contact-person records
- Periodic review of outdated information
- Reporting mechanism for incorrect listings
- Removal or suspension of unreliable listings
- Administrative review before an organisation becomes a referral partner

## 8. Basic impact and monitoring dashboard

The MVP could track non-identifying indicators such as:

- Number of verified organisations
- Number of support requests
- Number of referrals made
- Referral response time
- Referral completion rate
- Number of active partners
- Types of support accessed
- User feedback
- Partner feedback
- Privacy incidents
- Safeguarding incidents
- Unsuccessful or redirected referrals

## 9. Accessibility and language readiness

- Mobile-friendly design
- Simple and clear language
- Accessible forms
- Screen-reader-conscious interface design
- High-contrast and readable layouts
- Regional-language readiness
- Low-bandwidth considerations
- Clear instructions for users with limited digital literacy

## 10. Basic security and accountability

- Secure authentication
- Role-based permissions
- Administrative access logs
- Data-access logs
- Input validation
- Secure handling of uploaded information
- Abuse-reporting mechanism
- Account suspension capability
- Basic threat modelling
- Privacy and security testing before any controlled pilot

# Future Roadmap

The following features are long-term possibilities. They would be explored
only after research, professional consultation, safeguarding preparation,
technical testing, and suitable partnerships.

## 1. REBUILD Community Coordinator Network

REBUILD could create a network of trained and verified local coordinators who
can provide practical assistance to survivors and families.

### Coordinator pathway

**Training → Verification → Certification → Supervised volunteering →
Continued review**

Potential training topics:

- Survivor dignity and agency
- Informed consent
- Confidentiality
- Trauma-informed communication
- Avoiding victim-blaming
- Safe bystander intervention
- Recognising urgent or high-risk situations
- Referral procedures
- Basic legal and government-support navigation
- Digital and physical safety
- Ethical boundaries
- Appropriate handling of information
- When to contact NGOs, professionals, or emergency services
- Safe communication and documentation
- Responsible handling of donations or supplies

### Possible coordinator responsibilities

With the survivor’s consent, trained coordinators could:

- Help identify suitable local support services
- Contact verified NGOs
- Assist with transportation
- Accompany survivors to approved organisations
- Help with forms and documentation
- Provide translation or communication support
- Deliver approved supplies
- Help access education, rehabilitation, or employment services
- Offer practical companionship
- Coordinate with partner NGOs
- Report safety concerns through approved channels

Coordinators would not act as:

- Counsellors
- Doctors
- Lawyers
- Police officers
- Emergency responders
- Independent case managers

They would operate under clear boundaries and, where possible, the
supervision of partner NGOs or qualified professionals.

### Coordinator safeguards

- Identity verification
- Appropriate background checks
- Mandatory training
- Code of conduct
- Limited access to survivor information
- No unsolicited home visits
- No sharing of exact locations without permission
- No private financial arrangements with survivors
- Reporting and complaint mechanisms
- Blocking and suspension procedures
- Regular review and refresher training
- NGO or professional supervision

---

## 2. Safeguarded financial assistance and donations

REBUILD could explore a system for supporting verified needs such as:

- Medical treatment
- Rehabilitation
- Safe accommodation
- Education
- Vocational training
- Legal expenses
- Essential living costs
- Assistive devices
- Transportation
- Emergency necessities

Possible features:

- Funding requests verified by partner NGOs
- Donations managed through trusted organisations
- Purpose-based contributions
- Optional donor anonymity
- Public or private donor attribution
- Human review before disbursement
- Transparent records of amounts received and disbursed
- Anonymised financial reporting
- Fraud-prevention measures
- Donor receipts
- Complaint and dispute procedures
- Refund procedures where appropriate
- Impact updates without exposing survivor identities

Survivors would not be required to publish their names, photographs, or
personal stories to receive assistance.

---

## 3. Financial transparency and blockchain research

Blockchain may be researched as a possible tool for:

- Tamper-evident donation records
- Disbursement audit trails
- Verification of selected financial events
- Public aggregate financial reporting
- Cryptographic proofs of records
- Increased accountability between donors and partner organisations

However:

- Survivor names must never be stored on a public blockchain.
- Survivor photographs must never be stored on a public blockchain.
- Medical details, addresses, case histories, and identifying information must
  remain private.
- Sensitive data must be stored in secure, access-controlled systems.
- Blockchain should only be used if it solves a clearly identified problem.

A conventional secure and auditable database would likely be used first.

## 10. Public safety and policy research

REBUILD may eventually explore research and advocacy related to:

- Survivor-protection mechanisms
- Protection from stalking and retaliation
- Safer reporting systems
- Rehabilitation and compensation policies
- Institutional accountability
- Community-based support models
- Legally authorised offender-monitoring systems

# Proposed Development Approach

## Phase 1: Research and ecosystem mapping

- Study existing organisations and support systems
- Consult NGOs and trained professionals
- Map available services
- Identify gaps and duplication
- Understand safeguarding risks
- Define what REBUILD will and will not do

## Phase 2: MVP design

- Define the first user journey
- Create user roles
- Design wireframes
- Define data-protection rules
- Establish consent procedures
- Define referral categories
- Develop verification criteria

## Phase 3: Prototype development

- Build the support-request system
- Develop the verified directory
- Build the NGO dashboard
- Implement referral workflows
- Add basic authentication and access controls
- Create test data
- Avoid using real survivor information during early testing

## Phase 4: Testing and partner preparation

- Conduct usability testing
- Perform privacy and security testing
- Review the platform with professionals
- Develop referral procedures
- Verify potential partner organisations
- Test high-risk scenarios through simulations
- Prepare training and onboarding materials

## Phase 5: Controlled pilot

- Work with a small number of trusted organisations
- Use a limited geographic area
- Test supervised referrals
- Collect partner feedback
- Monitor response and completion rates
- Identify safety and usability problems

## Phase 6: Evaluation and next-stage planning

- Evaluate the pilot
- Measure referral outcomes
- Review safeguarding performance
- Publish anonymised findings
- Improve the prototype
- Formalise suitable partnerships
- Decide which future features should be prioritised

# Expected Social Impact

REBUILD aims to make trustworthy support easier to find and safer to access.

Potential impact includes:

- Easier access to reliable information
- Faster and more appropriate referrals
- Better coordination between organisations
- Improved access to medical, legal, and psychological support
- Greater access to rehabilitation
- Increased education and employment opportunities
- Safer and more accountable financial assistance
- Reduced dependence on unreliable information
- Greater awareness of survivor rights
- Reduced victim-blaming and stigma
- Stronger community participation through trained coordinators
- Greater feelings of support, dignity, and agency

The intended impact is not simply to provide information. It is to help
survivors feel that rebuilding their lives is possible.

A meaningful sign of success would be when a survivor who once felt that their
life had ended can say:

> “I want to live again.”

- Privacy and confidentiality
- Safety before convenience
